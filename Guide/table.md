## 1. What is the Floorsheet Table and Why Does It Matter?

While summary charts show where the market has been, the **Floorsheet Table** (`/table`) reveals the market in motion. Every executed trade generates a contract showing:
- **Who bought** (Broker ID)
- **Who sold** (Broker ID)
- **What stock** (Symbol)
- **How many shares** (Quantity)
- **At what price** (Rate)
- **Exact time** (Timestamp down to the second)

### Why Retail Traders Need This:
- **Spot Institutional Footprints**: Retail orders are typically 10 to 500 shares. When you see transactions of 5,000, 10,000, or 50,000 shares (Rs. 10 Lakhs to Rs. 1 Crore+), you are watching institutional "Smart Money" at work.
- **Uncover Secret Counterparties**: Did Broker #58 buy from Broker #45, or did they buy from their own clients? Counterparty transparency reveals institutional intent.
- **Detect In-House Crossings**: Find internal matched deals where big promoters or funds transfer large blocks without triggering open-market volatility.

## 2. Core Concepts & Data Fields

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Contract ID | Symbol | Buyer Broker  | Seller Broker | Quantity | Rate (NPR) | Amount (NPR) | Trade Time |
+-------------+--------+---------------+---------------+----------+------------+--------------+------------+
| 20260901... | NABIL  | #58 (Naasa)   | #45 (Imperial)| 2,500    | 585.00     | 1,462,500    | 14:12:05   |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

| Field Name | Description | Practical Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Contract ID** | Unique transaction identifier generated by NEPSE. | Proves the official authenticity of the record. |
| **Symbol** | Stock ticker (e.g. `NABIL`, `SHIVM`, `CHCL`). | Clicking opens the full [Symbol Analysis](symbols.md) dashboard. |
| **Buyer Broker** | The brokerage representing the purchasing party. | Marked with a green badge (`#58`). Clicking opens [Broker Analysis](brokers.md). |
| **Seller Broker** | The brokerage representing the selling party. | Marked with a red badge (`#45`). Clicking opens [Broker Analysis](brokers.md). |
| **Quantity** | Total units exchanged in this single trade. | High quantity at support levels indicates aggressive demand. |
| **Rate** | Price per share in Nepali Rupees (NPR). | The exact transacted price. |
| **Amount (Turnover)**| `Quantity × Rate`. | Values ≥ Rs. 1,000,000 (10 Lakhs) represent institutional whale trades. |
| **Trade Time** | Timestamp when NEPSE matched the buy and sell orders. | Essential for correlating price action with market hours (11:00 AM – 3:00 PM). |

## 3. Step-by-Step Walkthrough of Features

### A. Smart Filter Toolbar

Located directly above the table inside a glassmorphism container:

```
[ Symbol: e.g. RIDI ] [ Broker ID: e.g. 58 ] [ From: YYYY-MM-DD ] [ To: YYYY-MM-DD ]
[ ⭐ Watchlist Only ] [ 🌊 Whales (≥ 10L) ] [ 🔄 Crossings Only ] [ 🔄 Refresh ] [ 📥 Export CSV ]
```

1. **Symbol Filter**: Type any stock ticker (e.g., `SHIVM`). The table immediately filters to that symbol without reloading the page.
2. **Broker ID Filter**: Enter a broker number (e.g., `58`). Matches any trade where broker #58 was either the **buyer** OR the **seller**.
3. **From Date & To Date**: Filter historical trades to a specific date range. *(Note: You can also use the sticky **Global Date Range Bar** at the top of the screen (`1D`, `3D`, `7D`, `15D` default, `30D`, `90D`, `All Time`, Custom) to synchronize dates across all platform tabs!)*

---

### B. Power Toggles (Watchlist, Whales, Crossings)

Three instant-toggle buttons allow you to isolate critical trades with a single click:

- ⭐ **Watchlist Only**:
  - Filters the table to only show trades involving tickers or brokers you have starred in your persistent Watchlist bar.
  - Perfect for monitoring your personal portfolio without market noise.

- 🌊 **Whales (≥ 10L)**:
  - Filters out retail noise and displays **only mega transactions where Amount ≥ Rs. 1,000,000**.
  - Essential for identifying high-net-worth individuals and institutional block activity.

- 🔄 **Crossings Only**:
  - Displays only trades where `Buyer Broker == Seller Broker` (e.g., Buyer #58 and Seller #58).
  - Reveals internal matching, block transfers, and promoter stake shifts.

---

### C. Interactive Table Columns & Cross-Navigation

Every row in the table is an interactive springboard:
- **Clicking any Symbol** (e.g. `NABIL`): Instantly navigates to `/symbols?symbol=NABIL` with historical price charts, broker accumulation breakdowns, and volume stats.
- **Clicking any Buyer Broker** (e.g. `#58`): Opens `/brokers?broker=58` showing their total daily turnover, top holdings, and accumulation vs. distribution status.
- **Clicking any Seller Broker** (e.g. `#45`): Deep-links to `/brokers?broker=45` to see why that broker is liquidating shares.
- **Sorting**: Click any column header (`Trade Time`, `Amount`, `Rate`, `Quantity`) to sort ascending or descending.

## D. Server & Client-Side Pagination

The database holds millions of transaction records. The table employs a hybrid pagination architecture:
- **Server-Side Pagination**: Navigate across the 2.6M+ historical database records page by page.
- **Client-Side Slicing**: 50 records rendered per page for smooth 60fps scrolling and rapid client-side sorting.
- Use **Next** (`>`) and **Previous** (`<`) buttons to effortlessly page through history.

---

### E. 1-Click CSV Data Export

- Click the **Export CSV** button in the top right.
- Downloads the currently filtered transaction list into a structured `.csv` file.
- The exported file includes contract IDs, symbols, broker IDs, quantities, rates, amounts, and timestamps, ready for import into Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets, or Python pandas.

## 4. Real-World Example: Investigating a Whale Absorption

Suppose the market is pulling back, but you notice unusual buying interest in **SHIVM**:

1. Open `/` (Floorsheet Table).
2. Enter `SHIVM` in the **Symbol** filter box.
3. Click the **🌊 Whales (≥ 10L)** button.
4. **What You Observe**:
   - 6 consecutive transactions of 2,000 shares each at Rs. 510 executed between 13:45 and 13:52.
   - **Buyer**: Broker #58 on all 6 trades.
   - **Sellers**: Broken up across Broker #14, #34, #28, #42.
5. **Conclusion**:
   - Broker #58 (Naasa) is aggressively absorbing supply from scattered retail sellers at Rs. 510.
   - Rs. 510 is serving as strong institutional support!

## 5. Actionable Reading Patterns (Whales, Icebergs, Crossings)

| Pattern | What You See on the Table | Market Interpretation | Actionable Insight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Whale Inflow** | Single trade ≥ Rs. 10 Lakhs with strong buy-side broker. | Large player taking an outright stake. | Follow the buying broker in [Script Analysis](scriptanalysis.md). |
| **Iceberg Absorption** | Rapid cluster of medium trades (e.g. 500 shares) at identical rate from the same buyer. | An algorithm or broker dealer splitting a massive order to hide footprint. | Accumulation zone established. |
| **Internal Crossing** | `Buyer == Seller` for a large round share count (e.g., 50,000 units). | Internal client-to-client transfer or promoter restructuring. | Usually price-neutral, but signals liquidity changes. |
| **Retail Panic Dump** | Hundreds of tiny trades (10–50 shares) sold to 1 or 2 concentrated brokers. | Retail investors capitulating while smart money quietly collects shares. | High potential for a relief bounce. |

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: Why does a transaction show the same broker on both sides?
**A:** This is called an "Internal Crossing" or "In-house Match". It happens when one client of Broker #58 places a sell order and another client of Broker #58 places a buy order at the exact same price. NEPSE matches them directly.

### Q2: How far back does the historical data go?
**A:** The database stores all trades from July 2026 through September 2026. Use the Date Pickers or Server Pagination to travel back to any trading session.

### Q3: What is considered a "Whale Trade" in NEPSE?
**A:** While average retail orders are under Rs. 50,000, transactions exceeding **Rs. 1,000,000 (10 Lakhs)** represent significant capital commitment and are categorized as Whale Deals.
